Review

Reading the other gallery

"Touch" is a serious piece of work: four volumes, 80 exhibits, real spring physics and a "Why" line on every one. I read all four files closely, the prose and the code. This is what it gets right, where it stops short, and how this gallery answers.

What it does well

  • Physics over durations. Springs are interruptible and frame-rate independent. That is the right foundation, and this gallery keeps it.
  • Every exhibit justifies itself. The one-line "Why" is a good habit. Some are admirably honest, such as admitting that research on skeleton screens is mixed.
  • Craft in the hard ones. The page curl uses the real fold geometry. The swipe deck carries release velocity into the fling.
  • Restraint notes. "Use sparingly, once per flow" on the disintegrate effect shows taste.

Where it stops short

  • A formula in place of an insight. By Volume IV every pitch is a borrowed object: seesaw, hourglass, zipper, snow globe, tape measure, envelope, wax seal. Costumes age fast. Several also turn a one-tap action into a multi-second ritual, which is a cost on anything people do daily.
  • It preaches accessibility it doesn't practise. The notes say every custom control gets Semantics. The pages contain no tabindex, no role and one keyboard handler across all 80 exhibits. Native buttons still focus, but the dials, sliders, decks and canvases can't be reached or operated without a finger or a mouse.
  • It fights the page. 42 touch-action:none rules capture vertical drags, so the footer has to tell you to "scroll the page by dragging on the captions". On a phone, the gallery breaks the most common gesture there is.
  • Recipes are sketches. The Flutter recipes average 4.8 lines, for example "ScaleGestureDetector: scale 1.35 on a tile → level++". You can't estimate or build from that.
  • It repeats itself. Seven exhibits are "hold to do X". Three hide content behind a physical cover (fold, zip, scratch). Two set a value by circular drag. "Nothing repeated" holds for names, not for mechanics.
  • It ignores the viewer. No volume reads prefers-color-scheme. Only one volume styles keyboard focus at all, and the claim of "feedback in one frame" can't be checked anywhere on the page.

How this gallery answers

  • Borrowed objects as the ideaPrinciples as the idea: foresight, memory, candour. Each exhibit keeps the control people already know and adds one capability.
  • Rituals that slow frequent actionsTap still works everywhere. The new behaviour waits for the finger that reaches further: a longer drag, a held press, a faster flick.
  • Accessibility in the notes onlyAccessibility in the code. Every exhibit has keyboard operation, roles, values and live announcements, and each lists exactly how it works.
  • Captured scrollControls claim only the axis they use. The page always scrolls.
  • Claims you can't checkX-ray draws the touch area, velocity vector, recognised gesture, the exhibit's decision and every haptic, named as its Flutter call.
  • Recipes as hintsReal widget code with the actual APIs (SpringSimulation, ScrollPhysics, Semantics, CustomPainter), plus an APK blueprint below.
