A Flutter game concept · steps and calories are the engine

Walk, and the trail wakes up.

Trailkin is a pocket-sized walking game. Every 700 steps a little creature rustles in a bush along your trail. Your active calories weave the nests you catch them with. The closer you get to your daily goal, the rarer the kin, and a rare one is always waiting at the summit.

  • Tap a rustling bush on the trail, then flick the nest up to catch the kin inside.
  • Tap a glowing waystone to crack it open for a small reward.
  • Use the walk simulator to feed the game step and calorie data, the way Health Connect would on a phone.

The loop

Two numbers from your phone run the whole game

Trailkin reads exactly two things: steps and active calories. It never needs your location, a map or a camera, so it works on a treadmill, in a city, in the rain, and it can't send anyone into traffic. Each data stream feeds one part of the game.

Steps

Kin appear and waystones light

A creature rustles in a bush along your trail every 700 steps. Eight waystones mark the way to your goal, and the first ones come quickly.

1 kin / 700 steps · waystones at 6, 15, 25, 38, 50, 63, 78, 100 % of goal

Active calories

Nests get woven

Nests are what you throw. Every 40 active kcal weaves one. A brisk walk fills your hands; a lazy day leaves you a few short, which is a gentle reason to move.

1 nest / 40 active kcal · unused nests carry over

Daily goal

Rarity rises as you climb

Common kin live near the start. Uncommon ones appear past halfway. A rare kin waits at the summit, visible as a silhouette from your first step, and comes down when you reach your goal.

<50 %: common · 50–100 %: 35 % uncommon · goal: 1 rare · 125 %: elusive

The week

The lantern keeps the habit

Each goal day lights an ember. A missed day lets one go out, but the first miss each week is covered by a rest ember. Keep five lit and a rare visitor arrives on Sunday.

7 embers · 1 rest ember / week · ≥5 lit → Sunday visitor

Seven short rewards

Every interaction pays off in under ten seconds

People open a walking app for a few seconds between other things. Each moment below is complete in that time, has its own haptic signature, and has one job in the habit loop. Press Play it to see each one in the prototype.

Welcome back

2 stick per waystone

Open the app after a walk and the steps you took pour onto the trail. The counter rolls up, your buddy walks the path, each waystone you passed lights with a tick, and new bushes pop up where kin are waiting.

Why: step data arrives late and silently. Replaying it turns a number you never saw change into a walk you can watch.

Flick a nest

5–10 srelease · wiggles · success

Press the nest and flick it up. Speed sets the distance, a sideways flick adds curve, and a kin that pauses to blink is easier to catch. A throw that falls short rolls back for free.

Why: you control the aim and luck decides the rest. That mix of skill and chance makes a catch feel earned and keeps it surprising, with nothing for sale.

Crack a waystone

3 tapslight → medium → heavy

Each waystone you reach glows. Three taps crack it open: a spare nest, a scent trail that makes your next kin uncommon, a field note about an undiscovered kin, or a treat for your buddy.

Why: the first stones sit close together, so every day starts with quick wins. Progress that has already begun is the progress people finish.

Reach the summit

3 ssuccess

Crossing your goal floods the screen with warm light, the number lands like a stamp, and the rare kin you've seen as a silhouette all day comes down to meet you.

Why: showing the prize from the first step makes the last thousand steps feel shortest. People speed up as a goal gets close; the summit uses that pull.

Your buddy asks

one glancelight

After a long still stretch, your buddy peeks in from the edge with one concrete offer: "The next waystone is 620 steps away. That's about 6 minutes. Walk with me?" Saying yes starts a 10-minute stroll with an uncommon kin at the end.

Why: a small, specific plan beats a vague target. Steps are turned into minutes, and the reward is named before you stand up. There is no guilt and no red badge.

Light the ember

1.2 s holdrising buzz → success

On a goal day, hold the button and today's ember catches: a spark, a rising buzz, then a steady flame in the weekly lantern. Your Sunday visitor waits in the middle as a silhouette until five embers burn.

Why: losing something you already hold motivates more than winning the same thing. The lantern starts the week full of ember slots and you protect them, with one free miss so a single bad day never breaks the week.

Stamp the journal

1 smedium thud

A new kin lands in the field journal with an ink stamp, and its page remembers the walk: "Found Thursday at 4,820 steps, Perfect throw". Any kin you've found can become your buddy and walk the trail with you.

Why: a collection gives each walk a lasting trace. The entry records when and how far you walked, so the journal doubles as a diary of your walks.

Research

What the evidence says, and what Trailkin does about it

Location games proved that play can get people walking. They also showed the effect fades, and that a game pulling people into streets has a cost. Each design decision here answers a specific finding.

+955 steps

The boost is real, then fades

Pokémon GO players walked about 955 more steps a day in their first week. By week six they were back to where they started.

Howe et al., BMJ 2016 · summary

So in TrailkinNothing depends on novelty. Rarity is tied to your own goal and your week, the buddy is a kin you chose, and each day takes seconds, not sessions.
+25 %

Games reach people health apps don't

Engaged players added about 1,473 steps a day, over 25 % more than before, and the game reached low-activity people that leading health apps mostly missed.

Althoff, White & Horvitz, JMIR 2016 · paper

So in TrailkinYour goal starts from your own recent days: the median of the last 14 plus 10 %, raised by at most 500 steps a week. A beginner's summit is reachable on day one.
+920 steps

Points you can lose work, and competition lasts

In the STEP UP trial, adults who started each week with points and lost some for every missed goal walked about 920 more steps a day. The competitive version was the only one still ahead after the game ended.

Patel et al., JAMA Internal Medicine 2019 · abstract

So in TrailkinThe weekly lantern is framed as loss: seven embers to protect. A friendly two-person "trail race" is the next feature on the roadmap.
34 % vs 19 %

A head start gets finished

Car-wash cards needing 8 stamps, or 10 with 2 already stamped, demand the same effort. The pre-stamped card was completed almost twice as often.

Nunes & Drèze, Journal of Consumer Research 2006 · summary

So in TrailkinWaystones are front-loaded. The first two come at 500 and 1,200 steps, so most days you've passed two before breakfast is over.
+26.5 %

Location play has a safety cost

After launch, crashes within 100 m of PokéStops in one Indiana county rose 26.5 % more than elsewhere (working paper).

Faccio & McConnell, 2017 · coverage

So in TrailkinNo map, no location, no timers. Kin wait on the trail indefinitely, and throwing pauses while your step cadence says you're walking. You play when you stop.
8,000

The right target is not 10,000

Across 15 cohorts and 47,471 adults, mortality risk kept falling up to about 6,000–8,000 steps a day for people over 60, and 8,000–10,000 for younger adults.

Paluch et al., Lancet Public Health 2022 · paper

So in TrailkinThe default summit is 8,000 steps and the adaptive goal stops climbing at 10,000. Past that, the game rewards consistency, not more steps.

No pay-to-catch

Nests come only from active calories. There is nothing to buy that a walk can't give you.

No streak that resets to zero

A missed day costs one ember, and the first miss each week is free. The week never falls apart over a sick day.

Steps for every body

Wheelchair pushes from Health Connect and HealthKit count as steps, and goals adapt to anyone's baseline.

Flutter

How it becomes an APK

The prototype is built from the same pieces the Flutter app needs: a data layer for steps and calories, a pure-Dart economy, a few painters and one physics simulation. Your band already syncs to Health Connect through Mi Fitness, so Trailkin can read the same step and calorie records with no extra setup.

Data: Health Connect is the source of truth

Daily totals come from Health Connect on Android and HealthKit on iOS through the health package. The live phone sensor is used only to animate steps during a session, never added to the totals, so nothing is counted twice.

class ActivityRepository {
  final _health = Health();

  Future<void> init() async {
    await _health.configure();
    await _health.requestAuthorization(
      [HealthDataType.STEPS, HealthDataType.ACTIVE_ENERGY_BURNED],
      permissions: [HealthDataAccess.READ, HealthDataAccess.READ],
    );
  }

  Future<DayActivity> today() async {
    final now = DateTime.now();
    final midnight = DateTime(now.year, now.month, now.day);
    final steps = await _health.getTotalStepsInInterval(midnight, now) ?? 0;
    final energy = await _health.getHealthDataFromTypes(
      types: [HealthDataType.ACTIVE_ENERGY_BURNED],
      startTime: midnight, endTime: now,
    );
    final kcal = _health.removeDuplicates(energy).fold<double>(0,
        (sum, p) => sum + (p.value as NumericHealthValue).numericValue.toDouble());
    return DayActivity(steps: steps, activeKcal: kcal.round());
  }
}

Economy: pure Dart, fully testable

Every rule from "The loop" lives in one immutable class with no Flutter imports. Unit tests replay whole weeks in milliseconds, and the rules can be tuned remotely without a release.

@immutable
class TrailRules {
  const TrailRules({this.spawnEvery = 700, this.kcalPerNest = 40,
      this.maxWaiting = 6, this.wayFractions = const [
        .0625, .15, .25, .375, .5, .625, .775, 1.0]});
  final int spawnEvery, kcalPerNest, maxWaiting;
  final List<double> wayFractions;
}

class TrailEconomy {
  TrailEconomy(this.rules, this.state);
  final TrailRules rules;
  TrailState state;

  /// Applies new totals and returns what happened, for the step-in animation.
  StepInEvents apply(DayActivity a) {
    final spawns = <Encounter>[];
    while ((state.spawned + 1) * rules.spawnEvery <= a.steps) {
      state = state.copyWith(spawned: state.spawned + 1);
      if (state.waiting.length < rules.maxWaiting) {
        spawns.add(Encounter(pick(a.steps / state.goal), at: state.spawned * rules.spawnEvery));
      }
    }
    final crossed = state.steps < state.goal && a.steps >= state.goal;
    state = state.copyWith(steps: a.steps, kcal: a.activeKcal,
        waiting: [...state.waiting, ...spawns]);
    return StepInEvents(spawns: spawns, goalCrossed: crossed);
  }

  int get nests => state.kcal ~/ rules.kcalPerNest + state.bonusNests - state.usedNests;
}

The throw: one simulation, one painter

The flick's release velocity becomes a small 3D flight: depth, height, drift and curve. A Ticker steps it, a CustomPainter draws it, and the landing point decides the accuracy word.

class NestFlight {
  NestFlight(Velocity v)
      : vz = (-v.pixelsPerSecond.dy / 1800).clamp(0.0, 1.4),
        vx = v.pixelsPerSecond.dx / 900,
        curve = v.pixelsPerSecond.dx.sign * .35;
  final double vz, vx, curve;
  double z = 0, x = 0, y = 0, t = 0;

  bool step(double dt) {                  // true while airborne
    t += dt;
    z += vz * dt;                          // depth into the scene, 0..1
    x += (vx + curve * t) * dt;            // drift, plus the curve's pull
    y = 1.8 * z * (1 - z);              // parabola peaking mid-flight
    return z < 1 && vz > .25;
  }
}

GestureDetector(
  onPanEnd: (d) {
    if (d.velocity.pixelsPerSecond.dy > -600) return _rollBack(); // too soft: free
    HapticFeedback.lightImpact();
    setState(() => _flight = NestFlight(d.velocity));
    _ticker.start();
  },
  child: CustomPaint(painter: MeadowPainter(kin: kin, flight: _flight, t: _t)),

Nudges and safety

  • Background check: workmanager runs every 15 minutes, reads Health Connect and decides whether anything is worth saying. At most two notifications a day, never in quiet hours.
  • The buddy nudge fires only after 2 hours with fewer than 250 steps, and always names a concrete distance and reward: "Mossbit found a bush 600 steps away".
  • Walking detection uses the step-cadence stream: more than 15 steps in 20 s means moving, so the encounter screen pauses throwing.
  • Haptics use the same vocabulary as the prototype: selectionClick for ticks, lightImpact for throws and wiggles, a two-beat success for catches.
  • Accessibility: every gesture has a button twin (a well-aimed throw on Enter). Kin and stones are Semantics nodes. Reduced motion swaps flights for fades.
